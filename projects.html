Opportunity

The Land’escapes property, just south of Bancroft, Ontario, spans an incredible 26,300 acres (10,643 hectares) – twice the size of Frontenac Provincial Park. It is home to 20 completely undeveloped lakes, 13 species at risk (including the Canada warbler, and Blanding’s turtle), has over 5,000 acres of pristine wetlands, and provided habitat for wide-ranging mammals including moose, bear and elk.

The Land’escapes property, fondly referred to as “The Park” by owner Ben Samann, has been recognized as the largest protected area owned by an individual in Ontario, and Canada. The designation “protected area” is the same as that given to a provincial park or a private nature reserve. The Park has been entered into the Canadian Protected and Conserved Areas Database, meaning it is contributing to Canada’s target of protecting 30% of its lands and waters by 2030. The formal recognition of protected and conserved areas, without the need for a conservation entity or government to acquire title or rights to the land, is an innovative conservation tool that is significantly accelerating the pace and scale of conservation in Ontario and Canada.

Solution

Ben acquired the site in 2021 from a U.S.-based forestry company, with a vision to conserve, restore, and conduct leading edge research on the land. In coordination with conservation partners such as Ontario Conservation Accelerator, Blazing Star Environmental, and the University of Guelph, the land has undergone detailed management planning and inventory. These partnerships, in addition to conservation-focused property and forest management plans, municipal zoning recognizing environmentally sensitive and significant wildlife areas within the property, and long term planning for the property to remain in conservation beyond the life of the current owner all help ensure this site will be provide conservation outcomes for the long term.

This property plays a vital role in protecting two of Ontario’s most important wildlife corridors – The Land Between corridor and the Algonquin to Adirondack corridor. It will serve as an anchor for future conservation efforts in this region. Additionally, The Park aims to play a tangible role in reconciliation with First Nations, as the lands are available to access for Indigenous people upon request to Land’escapes Indigenous Engagement Coordinator.
